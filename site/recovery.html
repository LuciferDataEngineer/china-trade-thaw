What came back

By 2025, five of the eight blocked groups were back to at least 90 percent of their 2017–19 sales to China (Barley, Shellfish, Beef, Cotton and Copper ores). Wine and drinks and Coal were only part of the way back. Timber logs were still well short.

Barley: 2025 was 207% of the 2017–19 average

Extra tariffs from May 2020 were removed in August 2023. For Barley alone, recorded sales in the blocked years were $3.0 billion below the 2017–19 average. The $41.2 billion on the card above is every blocked group combined, not Barley alone. In the first nine months of 2026, sales were already $2.6 billion, above the whole of 2025.

What this means

“Back” means 2025 sales reached at least 90 percent of the 2017–19 average. During the blocked years, recorded sales across all eight blocked groups were $41.2 billion below that old average. Coal is most of it, about $26.5 billion. A comparison with goods that were not blocked is in the method note. It also moves for wool, which was not blocked, so these pages use the recorded average. China's wheat purchases rose from $103 million in 2019 to $3.2 billion in 2023, while barley was blocked. Wheat is not used as a stand-in for a normal year.
